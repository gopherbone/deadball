# Credits

## Photographs (face bank)

All face images in `playdate/source/images/faces/` are derived (cropped, resized and dithered to
1-bit) from photographs in the **George Grantham Bain Collection** (Bain News Service, c. 1900–1920s)
at the **Library of Congress, Prints & Photographs Division** (https://www.loc.gov/pictures/collection/ggbain/).

The Library of Congress lists the Bain Collection as having **no known restrictions on publication**
(https://www.loc.gov/rr/print/res/274_bain.html). The source item URL and original caption of every
photo used are recorded in `packages/sim/src/faces.ts` (`source`, `title`) and in
`playdate/source/images/faces/faces.json`.

The people in these photos are real, mostly obscure, people of the 1900s–1920s. In DEADBALL they are
used only as anonymous faces for fictional characters; the fictional names, deeds and misdeeds in the
game have nothing to do with them. Famous people (presidents, Hall of Fame ballplayers, celebrities)
are deliberately excluded (skip list in `tools/assets/faces_select.py`).

Download mirror: when loc.gov rate-limits, the pipeline fetches the same LoC scans from the Wikimedia
Commons copy of the collection (Category:George Grantham Bain Collection, files named by LCCN); every
photo is still credited to its LoC item.

Credit line: *Photos: Library of Congress, Prints & Photographs Division, George Grantham Bain Collection.*

## Tools

- Face detection for crop placement (offline, build-time only): YuNet face detector from the
  OpenCV Zoo (https://github.com/opencv/opencv_zoo), MIT License. Not shipped with the game.

## Everything else

All other art (puppets, ballparks, cards, UI bitmaps, logos, backgrounds) is generated
programmatically by `tools/assets/` for this project. Brand names on in-game billboards are fictional.

(rendered by the in-game Credits page). Rebuilt by `tools/audio/build.py` from
`tools/audio/selection.json`; raw downloads stay in `tools/audio/.cache/` (not shipped).

Rights rules: every RECORDING (not just the composition) is public domain or permissively
licensed. US recordings published before 1926 are public domain under the Music Modernization
Act (as of 2026). UCSB Cylinder Audio Archive states that its pre-1923 cylinder transfers are
free for any use, commercial or non-commercial. Works of US federal government bands (Marine
Band, Army Band, Air Force bands) are public domain. All compositions are pre-1930 or
traditional. No NC, ND or ShareAlike material is used.

### Our own arrangements and stingers (MIDI, `playdate/source/audio/midi/`)

Written for this project by `tools/audio/compose.py` and played on the Playdate through the
sampled combo organ above; the compositions are public domain:

- Take Me Out to the Ball Game — Albert Von Tilzer / Jack Norworth, 1908 (PD: published 1908). The whole chorus (32 bars) as a ballpark-organ waltz, transcribed note for note from the original 1908 York Music Co. piano-vocal score (Connecticut College, Greer Music Library, Historic Sheet Music Collection, Paper 1482: http://digitalcommons.conncoll.edu/sheetmusic/1482, mirrored at https://archive.org/details/sm_takemeouttotheballgame), with an intro from the score's own last phrase and first-ending turnaround and an original tag. Rendered offline (`tools/audio/organ_render.py`) as `take_me_out_organ.wav`.
- "Charge!" — traditional bugle-call fanfare (PD).
- Funeral March — Frédéric Chopin, Piano Sonata No. 2, Op. 35, 1840 (PD). Bars 1–4 of the march.
- Pop Goes the Weasel — traditional, 1850s (PD).
- Wedding March — Felix Mendelssohn, A Midsummer Night's Dream, 1842 (PD). Opening fanfare only.
- Bridal Chorus ("Here comes the bride") — Richard Wagner, Lohengrin, 1850 (PD). First phrase.
- "Shave and a Haircut" — traditional, from Charles Hale's "At a Darktown Cakewalk", 1899 (PD) — the ejection stinger.
- Original stingers and the stretch vamp (home run, strikeout, error, bet won/lost, pack opened, new mail, arrest) — composed for DEADBALL.

DBN broadcast stings (`playdate/source/audio/sfx/tv_*.wav`: network ident, whoosh, INSTANT REPLAY,
VHS rewind, SPECIAL REPORT bulletin, telestrator pen, data tick, freeze-frame, big-moment impact) —
original sounds synthesized for DEADBALL by `tools/audio/tv_stings.py` (numpy; no samples).

Synthesized sound effects (umpire "steee-rike" / "you're out" approximations, UI blips, chime,
fallbacks for every sampled effect) are generated live by `playdate/source/audio/sfx.lua`.

Modifications: every recording above was excerpted, mixed to mono, band-limited, resampled, loudness-normalized and IMA-ADPCM encoded for DEADBALL (organ notes: pitch-corrected and looped).

## Archive (news, incident and world-event pictures)

`playdate/source/images/archive/` holds 787 pictures (each at 160x100, 96x72 and 48x36; 340 also at 400x240), cropped, resized and dithered to 1-bit from public-domain photographs, engravings, natural-history plates, period advertisements and cartoons, plus a few CC0 and CC-BY images where nothing public-domain fit. 782 are public domain / CC0; 5 are CC-BY (attribution below).

Main sources: Library of Congress Prints & Photographs Division (Bain News Service, Detroit Publishing Co., National Photo Co., Harris & Ewing, FSA/OWI; "no known restrictions on publication"), and public-domain scans hosted on Wikimedia Commons (used as the search index and download mirror; LoC items are credited to their LoC item page). The people shown are real, mostly anonymous, people of their time; DEADBALL uses the pictures as generic illustration, and nothing in the game is about them. Famous people are filtered out.

Machine-readable: `playdate/source/images/archive/credits.json` (`{id, title, author, url, license}`; the in-game Credits page reads it) and `packages/sim/src/archive-data.ts` (`credit`, `source`, `license`).

## Puppet motion

The BallCast player puppets (`playdate/source/images/puppets/`) are rendered from the project's humanoid
base mesh (`tools/assets/humanoid/source/Humanoid.blend`): "Low Poly Human Mesh" by Comp-3 Interactive,
https://comp3interactive.itch.io/low-poly-human-mesh, CC0 1.0 (public domain); itself an optimised version of
Emerald Eel's Human Size Reference Model, credited here with thanks. Posed partly from motion capture:

- CMU Graphics Lab Motion Capture Database — http://mocap.cs.cmu.edu/ — subject 124, trial 01
  "Baseball Pitch" and trial 07 "Baseball Swing"; subject 9, trial 01 "run". "This dataset of motions is free
  for all uses… You may include this data in commercially-sold products, but you may not resell this data
  directly, even in converted form." The data used in this project was obtained from mocap.cs.cmu.edu.
  The database was created with funding from NSF EIA-0196217. Only retargeted key poses
  (`tools/assets/humanoid/mocap/keyposes.json`) are kept in the repo; the raw ASF/AMC files are downloaded
  into an uncommitted cache at build time and are not shipped.
